A WIN IS A WIN

Seattle answered the Washington loss with a 30-23 win, four takeaways, a revived run game and a 3-1 record. It also turned a 27-6 lead into a one-score finish and piled up 150 penalty yards. Good enough to bank the win. Plenty to clean up.

The game felt more comfortable than the final score until it suddenly did not. Seattle controlled the middle quarters, then spent the fourth quarter giving Los Angeles life before Uchenna Nwosu's strip-sack finally ended it.

What decided itThe good, the ugly and the game-sealer

The defense stole possessions

Seattle forced four takeaways. Drake Thomas and Ernest Jones IV intercepted Justin Herbert, Connor O'Toole recovered a muffed punt, then Nwosu forced the final fumble for Derick Hall to recover.

The run game came back

After only 44 rushing yards in Washington, Seattle finished with 138. Emanuel Wilson led the way with 81 rushing yards and scored both a rushing and receiving touchdown.

Fourteen penalties

Seattle was flagged 14 times for 150 yards. That is the main reason a game that looked like a blowout for most of three quarters became stressful late.

No fourth-quarter points

The Seahawks led 30-13 entering the final stretch and did not score again. Los Angeles cut it to seven and got the ball with a chance to tie before the strip-sack.

Were our 10 things right?Pregame reads graded after the final whistle

Five hits, three partials and two misses. The biggest wins were the run-game rebound, confusing Herbert and respecting the turnover battle. The biggest misses were discipline and closing the door.

Protect the football

Hit

Seattle threw one interception on its first offensive snap, then settled down. After three giveaways became 21 Washington points last week, finishing with only one giveaway was a major improvement.

02

How the Chargers cover JSN

Partial

Jaxon Smith-Njigba still produced 5 catches for 76 yards, but Los Angeles kept him out of the end zone and ended his run of 120-plus-yard games. He remained useful without owning the game.

03

Leonard Williams vs. Justin Herbert

Partial

The broader pass-rush read was right. Seattle sacked Herbert four times. Williams was not the centerpiece this week and left in the fourth quarter with an ankle injury, so this was more of a front-seven win than a Leonard Williams game.

04

Get the run game back

Hit

This one landed. Seattle rushed for 138 yards, Wilson had 81 on 21 carries, and Rashid Shaheed added a 28-yard touchdown run. The offense looked much more balanced.

05

Make Herbert solve the disguise

Hit

Herbert threw two interceptions, took four sacks and lost the game-sealing fumble. Seattle made him work for 189 passing yards and repeatedly changed possessions with defense.

06

Find Ladd McConkey on third down

Hit

McConkey finished with no catches before aggravating his foot injury and leaving the game. Injury context matters, but Seattle never let the Chargers' leading receiver become a factor.

07

Win in the red zone

Partial

Seattle finished drives well enough to build a 21-point lead, but Los Angeles scored two short touchdowns in the second half. The Seahawks won the game, not the entire late red-zone battle.

08

Respect the Chargers' takeaway ability

Hit

The warning showed up immediately when Darnold threw an interception one play after Seattle picked off Herbert. The difference was Seattle creating four takeaways of its own and winning the possession battle.

09

Do not give away hidden yards

Miss

Fourteen penalties for 150 yards made this the clearest miss on the list. Seattle kept extending drives and changing field position with flags.

10

Step on them if Seattle gets ahead

Miss

Seattle led 27-6 and later 30-13. The Chargers still had the ball down seven in the final minutes. The Seahawks won, but they did not finish the blowout that was sitting there.
