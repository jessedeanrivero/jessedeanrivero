Seattle's defense held Denver to 168 net yards. Seattle's offense produced 353. Somehow, the Broncos won anyway. An interception in overtime set up Wil Lutz's 53-yard game winner, and the Seahawks fell to 3–3.

Denver scored both its touchdowns in the second quarter on short runs by J.K. Dobbins and RJ Harvey. From there, Mike Macdonald's defense took over: Denver produced only 30 net yards in the second half and punted on every second-half possession.

Seattle still trailed 14–3 entering the fourth. George Holani scored from the 1, Sam Darnold found Rashid Shaheed for the two-point conversion, and Jason Myers tied it at 14 with a 34-yard field goal in the final seconds of regulation.

Then came the defining snap. Ja'Quan McMillian intercepted Darnold in overtime, giving Denver a short field. Wil Lutz ended it with a 53-yard kick. A game with 185 more Seattle net yards ended in another three-point defeat.

The Seahawks have now lost to Washington by two, San Francisco by three and Denver by three. Three losses, a combined eight points. The defense is creating winning conditions; offensive protection, giveaways and red-zone finishing keep wasting them.

War Room takeaway

Last week, Seattle gave San Francisco four turnovers. This week, it gave Denver three. Seattle held the 49ers to 269 yards and the Broncos to 168, yet lost both. The defense keeps putting this team in position to win. The offense needs to stop turning field position and scoring chances into opponent possessions.
