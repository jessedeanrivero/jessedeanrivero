SEAVSWAS

Two contrasting personnel stories. One early road test.

More muscle. More answers.

Seattle is 2–0 after holding New England and Arizona to 17 combined points. Washington is 0–2 after two NFC East losses and heads into its home opener with Marcus Mariota taking over for the injured Jayden Daniels. The matchup puts Brian Fleury's heavier Seattle offense opposite a Washington defense that just surrendered four Prescott touchdown passes.

  • Seattle won 31–7 in Arizona. JSN caught nine passes for 155 yards and three touchdowns, including an 82-yard score.
  • Washington lost 24–22 at Philadelphia and 37–20 at Dallas. The Commanders ran for 182 yards at Dallas, but gave up four passing touchdowns.
  • Watch what happens when Fleury uses a fullback and tight ends to force Washington into different personnel groups.

Jesse's personal note · Offensive identity

I called the heavy personnel.

“I love how Brian Fleury is getting the fullback involved more. During preseason I was on the phone with KC and told him I thought 21+ personnel was going to be big this year. I thought 13 personnel would have a role too. Seeing the early usage shift is exactly what I was hoping for.”

What interests me against Washington is what happens after we show the heavy look. Force a defense to respect the run, then make them defend JSN and the tight ends when the personnel stays on the field.

What to track on Sunday

Look for the fullback's role, not only his snap count. Lead blocker on first down, motion man before the snap, or a pass-game decoy? Those three uses force different defensive answers.

If Washington swaps into its base defense against 21, watch the immediate play-action matchup. If it stays in nickel, watch whether Seattle earns efficient runs. In 13 personnel, look for which tight end slips into the route and whether JSN's alignment creates space.

Football IQThe concepts behind Week 3

21, 22 and 13 personnel

The first digit counts running backs and the second counts tight ends. In 21, one of the two running backs is often a fullback. In 13, Seattle puts three tight ends on the field.

This week: identify how Washington matches the package and whether Seattle turns the look into efficient rushing or a favorable play-action throw.

Percentage points versus percent growth

If 21 personnel goes from 14.3% to 28.9% of snaps, the increase is 14.6 percentage points. That is a different measure from its relative percent growth.

This week: the displayed chart's biggest shift is 21, with much smaller gains for 13 and 22.

Simulated pressure

The defense shows multiple possible rushers, then sends fewer than it threatens. The aim is to change the offense's protection identification while retaining coverage players.

This week: against Mariota, track whether Seattle's changing picture creates hesitation without losing contain.

EPA per play

Expected Points Added estimates how much a play changes a team's expected points relative to down, distance and field position. Two-game scoring totals and EPA tell different stories.

This week: the scoring baseline above is intentionally simple. A full adjusted HQ model requires updated efficiency and quarterback inputs.

Four-minute offense

A late-game possession focused on keeping the clock running while gaining enough first downs to protect a lead. Passing remains part of the approach when the defense sells out against the run.

This week: Seattle's 10:14 touchdown drive at Arizona is the template to watch when Fleury goes heavy late.
