Different Quarterbacks. Same Division.A 31–7 Week 2 win does not guarantee a repeat on Sunday.

Drew Lock handled this matchup in September. He went 19-for-26 for 235 yards and three touchdowns, with no interceptions, and found JSN for an 82-yard score on his first pass. Sam Darnold now sits with quadriceps and rib injuries. Lock has an elbow issue but enters Sunday without an injury designation, and Jalen Milroe is preparing as the backup.

Arizona also changed its starting quarterback. Gardner Minshew entered last week's game against Dallas and sparked a 33–30 overtime win, throwing for 188 yards and three touchdowns. The Cardinals have won two of their last three and arrive with momentum despite a 3–5 record.

Seattle is coming off its least competitive loss of the season. Against Chicago, the Seahawks finished 1-of-11 on third down. The defense allowed touchdowns on all four Bears red-zone possessions. Fixing the short-yardage and red-zone problems matters more than relying on the last Cardinals score.

10 Things To WatchTen testable Week 9 scouting calls. We will grade all ten after the game.

01

DREW LOCK, ROUND TWO

The last time Lock started, he opened with an 82-yard touchdown to JSN and finished with 235 yards and three passing scores in a 31–7 win at Arizona. This time he is managing a right-elbow issue and playing behind a different line. Track his first 15 dropbacks, turnover-worthy plays and whether the passing game stays on schedule.

02

PROTECT THE LEFT SIDE

Charles Cross and Quenton Nelson are out. Grey Zabel returns to left guard and Anthony Bradford starts at right guard. Watch how Fleury protects the left tackle spot, handles Josh Sweat and keeps Lock from facing free runners. Grade sacks, obvious pressures and rushed third-down throws.

03

NO MORE EMPTY RED-ZONE TRIPS

Seattle has scored touchdowns on only 46.4% of its red-zone trips, 29th in the NFL. Watch the first two trips inside the 20. A touchdown on at least half of Seattle's red-zone trips is the target.

04

RED-ZONE DEFENSE MUST CHANGE

Opponents have scored touchdowns on 83.3% of their trips inside Seattle's 20. Chicago went four-for-four. Watch whether Macdonald's defense forces a field goal or takeaway before Arizona gets to the goal line.

05

JSN AND THE 1,000-YARD MARK

Jaxon Smith-Njigba enters with 64 catches, 985 yards and nine receiving touchdowns. In Week 2 he caught eight passes for 155 yards and three touchdowns against Arizona. Watch Arizona's safety help and whether Lock still feeds his top target. Fifteen more yards reaches 1,000.

06

MINSHEW'S SECOND ACT

Gardner Minshew came off the bench last week for 188 passing yards and three touchdowns in Arizona's overtime win at Dallas. Macdonald must disrupt the timing without letting Minshew escape to extend drives. Track third-down conversion and explosive pass plays.

07

MAKE ALLGEIER EARN IT

Tyler Allgeier ran for 147 yards against Dallas and now leads Arizona's ground game with Jeremiyah Love on injured reserve. With Leonard Williams and Brandon Pili out, Seattle's interior run fits are under pressure. The call: keep Allgeier below 90 rushing yards.

08

ACCOUNT FOR TREY MCBRIDE

Arizona's tight end is a high-volume middle-of-field target. With 66 receptions and 544 yards entering the week, McBride will test Seattle's linebackers and safeties. The call: fewer than eight catches and no red-zone touchdown.

09

PRESSURE WITHOUT THE USUAL FRONT

Uchenna Nwosu and Leonard Williams are out, along with Brandon Pili. Seattle needs Derick Hall, DeMarcus Lawrence and its remaining rushers to create disruption without selling out against Minshew. The test: at least two sacks or a takeaway forced by pressure.

10

WIN THE HIDDEN YARDS

Rashid Shaheed is out, and Arizona has line injuries of its own. Seattle cannot lose field position on poor returns, coverage lapses or avoidable penalties. The call: win turnover margin and avoid a special-teams giveaway.
