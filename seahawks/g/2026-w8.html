Another National TestFrom shutting down Kansas City to facing a top-three Chicago offense.

Mike Macdonald's group held Kansas City to 213 total net yards and three points last Sunday. That was a needed response after three Seattle losses decided by eight combined points. Against the Chiefs, Seattle gained 410 yards, converted 11 of 16 third downs and watched JSN produce another 150-yard game.

Chicago is 5–2 after a 30–24 win over New England and ranks third in yards per game (391) and eighth in points per game (25.7). Ben Johnson's offense mixes an effective ground game with receivers Luther Burden III and Rome Odunze. Seattle leads the league in yards allowed and passes for a league-low 144.4 yards per game.

War Room read: This is the next test of Seattle's defensive ceiling, but the offense needs to help. The Chiefs win included zero red-zone touchdowns in three opportunities and a Darnold end-zone interception. Against Chicago, turn those possessions into points.

10 Things To WatchTen testable pregame calls to grade after Monday.

01

CAN SEATTLE REPEAT THE DEFENSIVE STANDARD?

The Chiefs came in with the league's No. 1 offense and scored three points. Chicago enters averaging 391 yards per game, third in the NFL. Grade Seattle by explosive plays allowed, third-down stops, and whether Caleb Williams must drive the full field.

02

NO MORE RED-ZONE FIELD-GOAL DRIVES

Seattle went 0-for-3 scoring touchdowns in the red zone against Kansas City despite 410 yards of offense. Count red-zone trips and touchdown conversions. Long drives need six, not another collection of threes.

03

QUENTON NELSON'S FIRST IMPACT

Seattle traded for eight-time Pro Bowl guard Quenton Nelson on October 27. If cleared and active, follow his role in pass protection and the interior run game. Do not assume a start until the club confirms his availability.

04

SIMMONS CHANGES THE MATH

Chicago acquired All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons on October 29. If he plays Monday, Brian Fleury must account for his interior rush in protection. Watch how quickly Darnold reaches his first read.

05

JSN AGAINST A NEW DEFENSIVE LOOK

Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 867 receiving yards and eight touchdowns in seven games. Does Chicago shade coverage toward him? Track third-down targets, bracket adjustments and the other receivers' answers.

06

SWIFT BEFORE THE SECOND LEVEL

D'Andre Swift leads Chicago with 476 rushing yards and seven rushing touchdowns. Kyle Monangai has 324 yards on 60 carries, but his toe and thumb injuries merit monitoring. Seattle must keep its run fits sound before opening extra pressure packages.

07

CALEB'S EXTENDED PLAYS

Caleb Williams has completed 71 percent of his passes for 1,020 yards, five TDs and one interception. His hamstring history is worth tracking, but don't assume a limitation. Seattle needs four rush lanes and coverage discipline when a play breaks down.

08

BURDEN AND ODUNZE DOWNFIELD

Luther Burden III leads Chicago with 491 receiving yards. Rome Odunze adds 381 yards and a 15.9-yard average per catch. Seattle's secondary must tackle underneath without surrendering deep shots.

09

CROSS, SHAHEED AND THE MONDAY INACTIVES

Charles Cross left the Chiefs game with an ankle injury, Rashid Shaheed with a knee issue, and Velus Jones Jr. with a hamstring problem. Track the official practice reports and Monday's inactive list before locking in any lineup assumptions.

10

THE TURNOVER MARGIN

Sam Darnold stands at eight touchdown passes and eight interceptions, while the Seahawks have 13 offensive turnovers and 12 defensive takeaways. Against a ball-control Chicago offense, Seattle's margin for giveaways is small.

Macdonald vs. Ben Johnson

Johnson's offense emphasizes formation changes, run-pass conflicts and using its skill players in space. Seattle must communicate when motion changes leverage and resist overreacting to eye candy. Winning early downs lets Macdonald disguise more on third down.

Fleury vs. Chicago's front

Sam Darnold has eight interceptions in seven games. Chicago had 19 team sacks entering the week and added Jeffery Simmons. Protection and quick answers matter, especially if Charles Cross cannot play. Nelson's availability and position are crucial lineup questions.
