Week 7 · Postgame reportOctober 25, 2026 · Lumen FieldSeattle 4–3
The lights were bright. Seattle's defense was brighter.

TURNED OUT THE LIGHTS.

Kansas City arrived undefeated, leading the entire NFL with 414 offensive yards per game. Seattle dismantled that No. 1 offense, holding Patrick Mahomes and the Chiefs to 213 total yards and a single field goal in a 20–3 Sunday night statement. The Seahawks are 4–3, and the rest of the league has been reminded they're still a threat.

The previous two weeks were maddening. Seattle lost close games to San Francisco and Denver despite strong defensive stretches, with turnovers keeping a 3–3 team from building momentum. Against the Chiefs, the defense delivered its clearest answer yet. Kansas City entered the night 5–0 and first in the league in yards per game. It finished with 213 net yards, 11 first downs and only three points.

Mike Macdonald's defense held Mahomes to 165 passing yards, no touchdown throws and an interception. Nick Emmanwori chased Mahomes and affected the throw that Ty Okada picked off. Even Kansas City's rushing leader looked ordinary. Walker came in with an NFL-best 665 yards in five games. Seattle limited him to 36 on 14 attempts, and the Chiefs ran for only 50 yards total.

On offense, Darnold responded after two difficult weeks. He completed 20 of 28 passes for 257 yards and hit Jaxon Smith-Njigba for a 28-yard touchdown. JSN caught nine balls for 150 yards. Emanuel Wilson ground out 100 yards on 24 carries, then iced the result with a 21-yard fourth-quarter touchdown. Seattle piled up 410 total yards and controlled the ball for nearly 36 minutes.

It wasn't perfect. Darnold threw an interception in the end zone, and Seattle finished 0-for-3 on red-zone touchdown chances. That weakness needs attention before the next close game. But the larger message stands: this defense has a ceiling capable of stopping top offenses, and with fewer mistakes, this Seattle team belongs in the conversation.

The War Room verdict

A 4–3 record doesn't tell the whole story. All three Seattle losses came by a combined eight points. On Sunday, Seattle backed up its defense with long possessions, efficient third downs, a healthy run game and another explosive JSN performance. The Seahawks didn't need a last-second escape. They dominated an unbeaten opponent from start to finish.

Next issue to solve: three red-zone trips and zero red-zone touchdowns. The defense has earned a better finishing rate from the offense.
