Week 9 · NFC West · FinalNovember 8, 2026 · Lumen FieldSeattle 5–4
A win we needed. A defense we trust. Work left to do.

BETTER. NOT FINISHED.

I'll take 17–3 every day of the week. The defense was terrific, the running game finally showed some life, and Drew Lock didn't give Arizona the ball. That's a step forward. But nine penalties for 83 yards? We still have a lot to clean up.

THE WIN MATTERS. SO DOES HOW WE GOT IT.We said we wanted progress that would survive next week. Here's the case for it, and what still worries me.

First, this defense. Arizona went 89 yards on its opening possession and came away with a field goal. Then the defense gave up only 109 yards the rest of the day. Three sacks, eight tackles for loss and a fourth-and-goal stand. We asked Macdonald's guys to respond after Chicago. They absolutely did.

The offensive line looked more settled. After 81 rushing yards and three sacks allowed on Monday night, Seattle ran for 120 and didn't give up a sack. Zach Charbonnet gave us 77 yards on 16 carries. Jadarian Price added 24 on the ground and was our leading receiver with 54. There's a version of this offense where the backs help everybody else. Today was a decent look at it.

Drew Lock did exactly what I wanted. Nineteen completions on 28 attempts, 175 yards, a touchdown and no pick. We went 7-for-14 on third down after going 1-for-11 against Chicago. AJ Barner scored both touchdowns, one on a sneak and one on a seven-yard catch. JSN added seven catches for 51 yards, enough to pass 1,000 receiving yards for the third straight season. There is real stuff to like here.

But I said before the game I'd be disappointed if we won without showing sustainable execution. We're not there yet. Nine accepted penalties for 83 yards, another game with nothing on the scoreboard after the third quarter, and no official takeaway despite a couple of calls we have a right to question. The Cardinals managed three points, so our mistakes never got expensive. Better teams will make us pay for them.

The War Room verdict

Defensively, this looked like the group I believe in. Offensively, we saw real improvement in protection, third downs and the run game. But a 17-point day with nine penalties isn't the finished version of a contender. Enjoy the win. Keep demanding growth.

DID WE CALL IT?Each item includes the original pregame expectation, even if you never saw the prep sheet. Nine hits, one mixed. Original calls are preserved on the game prep page.

01

LET'S RUN THE BALL

HIT

We called: A real rushing game, with more yards than the 81 against Chicago.

Here's the reality: Seattle ran for 120 yards on 33 attempts, 39 more yards than last week. Charbonnet added 77 on 16 carries. Progress, even if 3.6 per attempt leaves room to grow.

02

THE LINE LOOKS MORE FAMILIAR

HIT

We called: No free runners at Lock on the opening drive, with Zabel at LG, Bradford at RG and Josh Jones at tackle.

Here's the reality: The first series went three-and-out, but Seattle allowed zero sacks all afternoon. Protection improved from the three sacks allowed against Chicago. That is encouraging, especially against a front with Josh Sweat.

03

LOCK DOESN'T NEED TO SAVE US

HIT

We called: No interceptions. Let Lock take simple throws and lean on the run.

Here's the reality: Lock went 19-of-28 for 175 yards, one touchdown and zero interceptions. Nothing heroic, and it didn't need to be.

04

FINISH A DRIVE

HIT

We called: Improve on the 46.4% offensive red-zone touchdown rate and stop settling for threes.

Here's the reality: Seattle scored touchdowns on both red-zone trips, both from AJ Barner. Two chances. Two touchdowns.

05

MAKE ARIZONA KICK

HIT

We called: Force at least one red-zone stop, with a field goal counting as a win.

Here's the reality: Arizona went 0-for-2 on red-zone touchdowns. The first drive ended in a field goal. The fourth-quarter trip ended at the goal line on Nick Emmanwori's breakup.

06

JSN HITS 1,000

HIT

We called: At least 15 receiving yards to take JSN past 1,000 for the season.

Here's the reality: He caught seven passes for 51 yards and reached 1,036 on the season. It is his third straight 1,000-yard year.

07

DON'T GIVE ALLGEIER THE MIDDLE

HIT

We called: Keep Tyler Allgeier below 90 rushing yards after his 147-yard Week 8 performance.

Here's the reality: Allgeier finished with 46 yards on 17 carries. The front held up without Leonard Williams, Uchenna Nwosu and Brandon Pili.

08

MCBRIDE CAN'T OWN THIRD DOWN

HIT

We called: Fewer than eight catches for Trey McBride and no touchdowns.

Here's the reality: McBride finished with seven receptions for 54 yards and no score. Emmanwori disrupted him on big downs, including the decisive fourth-and-goal pass.

09

FIND PRESSURE SOMEWHERE

HIT

We called: At least two sacks or a takeaway created by pressure despite defensive line injuries.

Here's the reality: Seattle recorded three sacks, with Byron Murphy II, DeMarcus Lawrence and Dante Fowler Jr. all involved. No official turnovers, but pressure showed up.

10

LOOK LIKE A TEAM GETTING BETTER

MIXED

We called: Win the turnover battle and stop giving away possessions through penalties or special teams.

Here's the reality: The turnovers finished 0-0. Seattle had nine penalties for 83 yards, compared with five for 43 against Chicago. We won by 14 and improved on third down, but sloppy football is still in the picture. The defense made the result comfortable. The offense still needs to become more consistent.

What carries forwardWe head to Las Vegas next week.

Protect the quarterback, without needing a perfect day

Zero sacks and a functional run game were good signs even with injuries. I want to see that protection hold against a different front.

Make the clean plays routine

Third-down conversions improved dramatically. Now stop giving them back with flags. That has to be our standard, not a wish.

Don't waste this defense

We're 5–4 because we've been inconsistent, not because we lack talent. A defense this good gives us a chance in every game. The rest of the team needs to keep growing into it.
