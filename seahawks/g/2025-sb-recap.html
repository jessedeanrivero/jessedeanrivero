The blueprint

Seattle held the best offense in football to 13 points, never turned it over, and won a Super Bowl with a 33% offensive success rate. That combination should be impossible. Here is how it happened.

Seattle did not win this game on offense. Thirty-three percent success rate, minus .05 EPA per play, five Jason Myers field goals. Darnold graded out at the 47th percentile, which is to say completely average.

What won it was a defense that took the ball away three times and an offense that gave it away zero. Maye finished at the 11th percentile of quarterback games this season, sacked six times, picked off twice in the fourth quarter, both out of no-huddle. Derick Hall and Byron Murphy each had two sacks and each graded in the 99th percentile.

The tell is in the box counts. Seattle ran into eight or more defenders on 37.5% of carries and kept running anyway, 27 times to Walker. That is not stubbornness, that is a team that trusted its defense to win a low-scoring game and refused to put the ball in the air where it could be taken away.

What carries forward
  • New England had no answer for the four-man rush. Six sacks without needing to blitz.
  • Barner is a red zone problem for anyone. Ninety percent of snaps and the only offensive touchdown.
  • The run game is still a question. Twenty-seven Walker carries at 5.0 a pop looks fine until you see the -0.15 EPA.

About the grades. A grade is a percentile. It compares one player's production in this game against every comparable performance in the season: 591 quarterback games, 3,501 skill-player games, 9,753 defensive games. A 90 means the player produced more than 90% of comparable outings. It is built from expected points added per opportunity, completion percentage over expected for quarterbacks, and recorded defensive events.

What a grade is not. It cannot see blocking, coverage, or route running, because public play-by-play does not record them. A receiver who ran a perfect route on a play the quarterback missed gets nothing here. Treat these as production in context, not as scouting.
