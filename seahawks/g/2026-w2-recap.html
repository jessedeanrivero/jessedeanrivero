The short versionSeattle said, give us the ball

Arizona tied it 7-7 on the first play of the second quarter. Seattle answered with the next 24 points, owned the clock, and turned the second half into a possession clinic.

Drew Lock finished 19-of-26 for 235 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught all three scores, including an 82-yarder on Seattle's opening possession. The defense allowed only 151 total yards, and Arizona managed five punts and an interception on its six full possessions after its touchdown.

The game-control stat

Seattle ran 67 plays to Arizona's 47 and held the ball for 36:33. The final touchdown drive lasted 10:14, went 70 yards in 16 plays, and ended the discussion.

Prep sheet vs realityWhat hit, what missed, what changed

The Week 2 sheet had ten live-game cues. Here is the postgame audit.

01

Arizona feeds Trey McBride

Partial hit

McBride caught eight passes for 41 yards and Arizona's only touchdown. Seattle gave him volume without giving him explosive damage. Julian Love also broke up a third-down throw to McBride to force a punt.

02

Brissett gets the ball out in rhythm

Hit, then solved

Arizona found one sustained early drive. After halftime, Seattle changed the picture and Arizona produced only 44 yards. Brissett finished with 95 passing yards, one touchdown and one interception.

03

Attack Arizona inside

Result hit

Seattle's front controlled the game and got home for sacks, including interior pressure from Leonard Williams and Jarran Reed. Public box-score data does not assign each pressure to a specific guard matchup, so the exact injury matchup remains ungraded.

04

Watch Seattle's snap count and possession

Nailed it

This became the clearest story of the game. Seattle flipped Week 1's low-volume offense into 67 plays and 36:33 of possession. Arizona ran only 47 plays.

05

Lock finds JSN early

Nailed the player

JSN scored from 82 yards on Seattle's second offensive play. The exact play-action trigger from the prep sheet is less important than the result: Lock found the matchup immediately.

06

Make the run earn another carry

Hit

Seattle ran 40 times. Emanuel Wilson carried 21 times for 91 yards and the run game helped close the game with the 16-play, 10:14 final touchdown drive.

07

JSN starts winning, Arizona adjusts

Nailed it

Arizona never found an answer good enough. JSN finished with nine catches, 155 yards and three touchdowns. The second answer barely mattered because the first one kept working.

08

Arizona stays on schedule

Did not happen

The Cardinals finished with 151 yards, 10 first downs and 3.2 yards per play. Seattle kept creating long-yardage possessions instead of letting Arizona live in second-and-short.

09

One-score fourth quarter, watch turnovers

Game moved past it

Seattle entered the fourth quarter up 17-7, then JSN scored again. Turnovers finished 1-1, so the Week 1 formula did not decide this one.

10

Short fields and special teams swing it

Danger erased

Jadarian Price's fumble gave Arizona the ball near midfield, exactly the kind of short-field moment the sheet flagged. Seattle's defense forced a quick three-and-out and erased it.

What was trueAnd what the prep sheet got wrong

True

  • Lock protecting the ball mattered.
  • JSN was the matchup Seattle needed to feature early.
  • Possession and snap count were central after Week 1.
  • Seattle's defense controlled Arizona once it forced obvious passing downs.

Not this time

  • Arizona's Week 1 offensive rhythm did not carry over.
  • Turnover margin did not decide the game.
  • The expected one-score divisional finish disappeared.
  • The short-field mistake did not become points.
