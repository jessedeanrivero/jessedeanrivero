Four wins alone do not settle it. The scoring margins make the stronger case: San Francisco has scored 122 points and allowed 64, a +58 differential. Three wins came by double digits. Arizona stayed within six, so there is already evidence of a competitive game beneath the undefeated record.

Why the start matters

Purdy has avoided a sack through four games. Seattle needs to disrupt a functioning passing game, not wait for protection to collapse on its own. His movement makes disciplined rush lanes as important as beating a blocker.

The next test is different

After Melbourne and three home games, this is San Francisco’s first visit to an opponent’s stadium this season. Lumen adds a communication test. That is a reason to watch the protection closely, not a reason to dismiss four wins.

Protection context: 49ers offensive-line review.

Our assessment: Treat San Francisco as a contender. Seattle has a credible route to winning through pressure, disciplined coverage and possession control. Expecting another blowout would skip the work required to produce one.

Same opponent. New test.What 13-3 and 41-6 tell us

January 3 · Seattle 13, SF 3

Seattle closed the 2025 regular season by winning the division and the NFC’s top seed. The score was modest, but the defensive control was decisive.

January 17 · Seattle 41, SF 6

The divisional-round rematch became a rout. Seattle dominated all three phases and advanced. Across those two meetings, the Seahawks won 54-9.

The repeatable part is the approach: disciplined run fits, tackles which end the play, disguised coverage and pressure without easy escape routes. The final margin is an outcome, not a starting assumption.

Macdonald’s new-year, new-team message fits here. In March, he framed 2026 as a fresh group with a fresh challenge. San Francisco also has Raheem Morris coordinating its defense, while Seattle’s offense operates under Brian Fleury. Personnel, health and how each staff attacks the matchup deserve more weight than a January scoreboard.

One key difference from the playoff game: the 49ers entered it without Fred Warner and lost George Kittle to an Achilles injury in the preceding round. Those absences shaped the matchup. This week’s prep needs to account for both players instead of projecting the same offensive and defensive limitations.

Where this game turnsMatchup analysis

Seattle defense vs. Shanahan

Make the first option late, then keep Purdy contained. Kittle and McCaffrey punish defenders who chase the backfield action and lose the route behind them. Seattle’s rush and coverage need to finish the same play.

Darnold vs. coverage numbers

Kupp’s preview describes a 49ers defense willing to rush fewer players and protect the deep field. Seattle needs patient completions, useful runs and routes which make underneath defenders choose between two threats.

JSN vs. the secondary

Move him across formations. If coverage follows, another receiver gains space. If it stays in zones, motion helps Darnold identify the handoff points. The read is coverage quality, not target count alone.

The possession battle

Seattle forced four takeaways against the Chargers but still needed a late stop. This week, protect the ball and turn defensive wins into sustained offense. A turnover advantage loses value when penalties hand back first downs.

10 things to watchKeep this open on game day

Scouting reads with observable checks. We will revisit them after the game.

01

Make Purdy reset his feet

The first rush needs to disrupt his timing. The second effort needs to contain him. A sack is useful, but forcing a late checkdown or an off-platform throw also changes the down. Reckless edge pressure gives him a clean escape.

Game-day check: Track sacks, throwaways and scrambles separately. Does pressure end the play or extend it?

02

Win first down against McCaffrey

Seattle needs second-and-long before disguises become a weapon. Fit the run with discipline, then tackle the checkdown before it becomes an easy first down. Selling out against the run leaves the play-action window behind the linebackers.

Game-day check: On each 49ers drive, note whether the first play gains five or more yards.

03

Find Kittle before the snap

Kittle creates a conflict between run support and coverage. Watch his alignment, motion and release. A late safety rotation only helps if Seattle still passes off the seam and crossing routes cleanly.

Game-day check: Count catches beyond the sticks and uncovered releases, especially off play action.

04

Make the fullback replacement prove it

Juszczyk’s expected absence changes who handles lead blocks, motion and pass protection. San Francisco plans to use its tight ends in those jobs. Seattle must identify the replacement’s assignment instead of assuming a two-back look disappeared.

Game-day check: Watch who lines up at fullback and whether Seattle follows motion without losing the cutback lane.

05

Take the completions Morris offers

Kupp described a defense willing to drop seven or eight and limit downfield throws. Darnold needs to stay patient. JSN attracts attention, leaving an opening for Kupp, the tight ends or the back underneath.

Game-day check: Count early-down completions which keep Seattle ahead of the sticks. Avoid forced throws into crowded windows.

06

Give the run game another useful week

Emanuel Wilson and the line supplied a needed response against the Chargers. The goal here is manageable down-and-distance, even without long runs. Heavy personnel only helps if Seattle remains credible throwing from the same look.

Game-day check: Watch whether Seattle reaches third-and-3 or shorter without needing a scramble or penalty.

07

Test the injured secondary with motion

Upton Stout and Marques Sigle missed Wednesday practice. Their availability and replacements matter for slot coverage, communication and tackling. Move JSN around and make the defense declare its responsibilities.

Game-day check: Look for late checks, defenders pointing after the snap, and free access to the inside.

08

Watch Leonard Williams on passing downs

Williams practiced on a limited basis after his ankle injury. Availability alone will not reveal his effectiveness. Interior push keeps Purdy from stepping through edge pressure and helps Seattle defend the run with fewer resources.

Game-day check: Compare the pocket and run fits with Williams on the field versus his rotation snaps.

09

Make third-down stops count

Seattle must finish possessions without extending them through penalties. Last week’s 14 penalties for 150 yards left too many chances for Los Angeles. Against this offense, a stop followed by a flag is another scoring opportunity.

Game-day check: Record conversions allowed on third-and-7 or longer and first downs surrendered by penalty.

10

Finish the fourth quarter with the ball

The Chargers game showed the danger of going quiet with a lead. Seattle needs positive early-down plays, secure exchanges and enough first downs to control the final possessions. A rivalry game does not require a reckless knockout throw.

Game-day check: On Seattle’s final three possessions, track first downs, giveaways and time taken off the clock.
