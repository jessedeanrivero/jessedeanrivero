What Seattle Must ProveTwo straight losses, another national stage, no room for giveaways.

Seattle had 185 more net yards than Denver last Thursday and still lost 17–14 in overtime. The Seahawks held the Broncos to 168 net yards and got beaten by three turnovers, including a Darnold interception in overtime. Kansas City is not the opponent to spot extra possessions.

Now comes the oddest subplot of the season: Kenneth Walker III, MVP of Seattle's Super Bowl LX win, returns as a Chief. He leads the league with 665 rushing yards through five games and is averaging 6.3 per attempt. Seattle's own running game has struggled to find the same consistency, and Zach Charbonnet will not return this week.

War Room note: This matchup is about control. Protect the ball, protect Darnold, force Mahomes to drive the full field and make the Chiefs earn every point.

10 Things To WatchSpecific calls we will grade after Sunday night.

01

NO FREE FOOTBALLS

Seattle gave away 10 possessions across its three losses, including seven giveaways against San Francisco and Denver. Track interceptions, fumbles and whether Darnold throws the ball away when the first read disappears.

02

WALKER’S HOMECOMING

Super Bowl LX MVP Kenneth Walker III is now Kansas City's leading rusher, with 665 yards and 6.3 per carry. Seattle’s run fits and tackling angles face a familiar runner in an unfamiliar uniform.

03

KEEP MAHOMES ON SCHEDULE? NO.

Mahomes is completing 66.5% of his passes with nine touchdowns and three interceptions. Seattle needs to force long-yardage situations and finish pressure without giving up scramble lanes or free second-reaction completions.

04

JSN IS THE ANSWER

Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 717 receiving yards and seven touchdowns. Watch Kansas City’s safety help, third-down coverage and whether Seattle finds JSN early without forcing late throws.

05

FIX THE FIVE-SACK PROBLEM

Darnold took five sacks against the 49ers and five more against Denver. Watch Anthony Bradford's expected return, protection calls and how quickly Brian Fleury gets the ball out against Steve Spagnuolo's pressures.

06

WHO RUNS FOR SEATTLE?

Zach Charbonnet is out despite full practices this week. Seattle’s backfield, led by Emanuel Wilson, needs enough successful early-down runs to keep the Chiefs from sending constant pass pressure.

07

CHIEFS TACKLE TROUBLE

Kansas City is missing Jaylon Moore to a concussion and Josh Simmons to injured reserve. Seattle’s edge rush has a chance to affect Mahomes, subject to the availability of Uchenna Nwosu and DeMarcus Lawrence.

08

THE RED ZONE GETS LOUD

The last two losses showed how costly squandered drives become. Count red-zone trips, touchdowns instead of field goals, and whether Seattle avoids penalties that erase goal-to-go opportunities.

09

SUNDAY NIGHT, FULL VOLUME

An unbeaten Chiefs team comes to Lumen Field for primetime. Track third downs and procedural mistakes on both sides. Home noise matters most when Seattle gives its defense an actual lead.

10

WHO WINS THE FINAL POSSESSION?

Seattle’s three losses have come by a combined eight points. Close-game execution, timeouts, field position and ball security will decide whether this turns into another painful late finish.

Macdonald knows Walker's strengths, but Reid knows how to turn predictable run fits into passing opportunities. Look for Seattle's defense to disguise pressure and keep its tackling assignments intact.

Fleury vs. Spagnuolo

Chiefs defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo will test Seattle's protections. The return of right guard Anthony Bradford would help, but Darnold needs answers when Kansas City forces the ball out before routes develop.
