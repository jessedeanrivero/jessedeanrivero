Week 8 · Monday Night Football · FinalNovember 2, 2026 · Lumen FieldSeattle 4–4
The defense bent. The offense stalled. The Bears finished.

THE STANDARD SLIPPED.

A week after smothering the NFL's No. 1 offense, Seattle got outplayed in every phase by Chicago. The Bears won 34–17, ending the Seahawks' 30-game stretch without a loss by more than four points. Seattle is 4–4. This was no razor-thin loss.

The first drive set the tone. Chicago marched 80 yards in 17 plays for a touchdown. The Bears gained 363 net yards against a Seattle defense that entered first in the league in yards allowed, converted 7 of 15 third downs and held the ball for 35:03. The Seahawks held Swift to only 69 rushing yards on 27 carries, but his three goal-line touchdowns were the difference when Chicago reached the finish line.

Seattle's offense couldn't stay on schedule. Sam Darnold finished 17 for 34 for 188 yards, two touchdowns and an interception, and the Bears sacked him three times. Seattle converted 1 of 11 third downs, ran for 81 yards and struggled to finish inside the 20. Jason Myers missed a PAT, had a field goal blocked and made one field goal. Too many chances disappeared.

JSN remains the unmistakable bright spot. His 43-yard touchdown just before halftime pulled Seattle to 13–9. He finished with six catches for 118 yards and a score, his seventh 100-yard game through eight games. But the rest of the passing attack combined for only 70 yards. Chicago forced Seattle to depend on one receiver while the other options produced little.

There was one moment to steal this game. DeMarcus Lawrence forced and recovered a Colston Loveland fumble. Seattle scored a Cooper Kupp touchdown and converted the two-point play to close to 20–17 with 11:46 left. Chicago responded with a 56-yard touchdown drive, then intercepted a tipped Darnold pass and cashed the short field for a third Swift touchdown. A three-point margin became 17.

The War Room verdict

We spent the first seven weeks talking about Seattle's losses being decided by inches. Monday wasn't an inches game. It was a third-down, red-zone and special-teams failure. Chicago's offense proved the defense isn't automatic, and the Seahawks' offense needs a second answer beyond JSN.

01

CAN SEATTLE REPEAT THE DEFENSIVE STANDARD?

MISS

Chicago piled up 363 total yards, converted 7 of 15 third downs and put together touchdown drives of 80, 69 and 56 yards. The standard from the Chiefs game did not carry over.

02

NO MORE RED-ZONE FIELD-GOAL DRIVES

MISS

Seattle finished with one touchdown in four red-zone visits. There was a made field goal, a blocked attempt and a turnover on downs. The red-zone problem survived another week.

03

QUENTON NELSON'S FIRST IMPACT

MIXED

Nelson started at left guard in his Seattle debut, with Grey Zabel sliding to the right. The availability question was answered, but Seattle gained only 81 rushing yards and allowed three sacks.

04

SIMMONS CHANGES THE MATH

HIT

Jeffery Simmons debuted for Chicago and immediately disrupted the pocket. He finished with six tackles, a sack and another tackle for loss. Seattle went 1 for 11 on third down.

05

JSN AGAINST A NEW DEFENSIVE LOOK

HIT

JSN accounted for 118 of Seattle's 188 passing yards on six catches, including a 43-yard touchdown. We flagged the need for other receivers to respond. The secondary production never arrived.

06

SWIFT BEFORE THE SECOND LEVEL

MIXED

Seattle held D'Andre Swift to 69 yards on 27 carries, a modest 2.6 per attempt. But Swift scored three rushing touchdowns, including both of Chicago's late fourth-quarter scores.

07

CALEB'S EXTENDED PLAYS

HIT

Caleb Williams went 24 for 34 for 247 yards, one touchdown and zero interceptions. Seattle managed one sack, and his efficiency kept Chicago's offense ahead of schedule.

08

BURDEN AND ODUNZE DOWNFIELD

HIT

Rome Odunze caught four passes for 87 yards and Luther Burden III caught seven for 56, including Chicago's opening touchdown. They combined for 143 receiving yards.

09

CROSS, SHAHEED AND THE MONDAY INACTIVES

HIT

Charles Cross and Rashid Shaheed were inactive, while Nelson, Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price played. Kyle Monangai was out for Chicago. The personnel check mattered before kickoff.

10

THE TURNOVER MARGIN

MIXED

Both offenses turned it over once. DeMarcus Lawrence's forced fumble led to Seattle cutting the lead to 20–17, but Darnold's tipped interception handed Chicago a six-yard field and its final touchdown.
